零、这套方案的"与众不同"之处（一句话）

别人会把这篇当"一篇 COVID 科普"来讲。你们把它当成一次工程鉴定评审（a qualification review）来讲。

整场汇报只回答一个问题：这套"家用口罩复用流程"，能不能通过验收？ 评审要过关，必须回答三问——① 病毒杀得死吗？② 口罩还滤得住吗？③ 真实戴过之后还行吗？ 最后给验收结论 + 明确写出"本方法未证明的部分"。

为什么这个角度值钱：① 它 100% 贴合论文本身的论证逻辑，不算"加戏"；② 这是工程师的母语思维——定验收准则、跑 N 个循环、检测、再放行——别人讲不出来；③ 成本为零，只需要换一套串场词；④ 结尾那 30 秒能自然落到你的燃气轮机专业上，成为全场唯一的记忆点。

注：不需要角色扮演、不需要道具、不需要互动环节。只是用了评审会的语言框架，形式仍然是最朴素的分段汇报。

五、五份英文稿（可直接用，也可改成你自己的话）

写法标准：能说出来的英文——短句、主动语态、数字清楚。粗体是必须重读的锚点词。方括号里填你们的信息。括号内小字是提示，不上台。

▍S1 · 开场 · 立问题（约 2.5 分钟）

Good morning, everyone. We are Group [X]. Today we'd like to talk about a very ordinary object — a disposable face mask — and one very practical question: can you reuse it?

In February 2020, this was not a small question. According to the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, China's total mask production was only 8 million a day on the 23rd of January 2020 — for a population of over 1.4 billion. The gap between supply and demand was enormous. So the question became urgent: is there a way to make one mask last longer — safely?

The paper we chose is called Can Masks Be Reused After Hot Water Decontamination During the COVID-19 Pandemic? It was published in 2020 in Engineering, the official journal of the Chinese Academy of Engineering. The first author is Dan Wang, from Beijing University of Chemical Technology.

One detail I want to point out: the paper was received on the 26th of May 2020, and accepted three days later. A three-day review is extremely unusual. That tells you how urgent this problem was.

The method they propose is called "hot water decontamination plus charge regeneration." In plain English: soak the mask in hot water, then dry it with a hair dryer. No chemicals, no special machines — you can do it in your kitchen.

Now, this paper is essentially a qualification test. Before you approve any reuse method, you have to answer three questions. One: does it kill the virus? Two: does the mask still filter? Three: does it survive real use?

My teammate [Name] will start with the procedure itself.

▍S2 · 流程本身（约 3 分钟）

Thank you. So let's start with the procedure itself.

The decontamination part is surprisingly simple. You pour boiling water into a container, put the mask in, and leave it for 30 minutes. The target is a water temperature above 56 degrees Celsius for 30 minutes. That number comes from the National Health Commission of China: at that temperature the envelope protein of the virus denatures, and the virus loses its ability to infect.

The researchers tested three everyday containers: an aluminium basin, a plastic lunch box, and a stainless-steel thermos. They measured the water temperature over time. Freshly boiled water was about 90 degrees. After 30 minutes, the basin and the lunch box were still around 60 degrees, and the thermos was still 85 degrees. So the temperature requirement is easy to meet at home, with things you already own.

They also compared this with other methods — UV light, organic solvents, hydrogen peroxide vapour. All of those work, but they need equipment. Hot water needs a pot.

There's a nice bonus. Under a 365-nanometre UV lamp, a mask worn for four hours shows fluorescent spots — those are wastes from the mouth and respiratory tract. After the hot-water treatment, the spots are gone, and so is the smell. So the method also cleans the mask.

Now, the clever part. Hot water kills the virus, but it also destroys the static electricity in the mask — and that static charge is what makes the mask work. So it has to be put back. The researchers measured this. If you simply let the mask air-dry for 10 hours, the static electricity recovers to about 60 percent of a new mask. But if you dry it with an ordinary hair dryer for 10 minutes, it recovers to about 90 percent — and much faster, which also prevents bacteria from growing inside a wet mask.

So the full recipe is: hot water, 56 degrees, 30 minutes; hair dryer, 10 minutes. Next, [Name] will ask the real question: does the mask still filter?

▍S3 · 滤得住吗（约 3 分钟）

Thank you. So — does it still filter?

First, a quick look at what a mask actually is. A typical mask has three layers. The outer layer is waterproof; the inner layer absorbs your breath moisture. The middle layer — the one that does the real work — is a polypropylene melt-blown fabric. It is 100 to 1000 micrometres thick, made of fibres just 1 to 10 micrometres across.

Now, how does it catch particles? There are five mechanisms: Brownian diffusion, entrapment, inertial collision, gravity sedimentation, and electrostatic adsorption. That last one is the key. It is the only mechanism that can capture nano-sized particles while keeping the mask easy to breathe through. And it is exactly the one that hot water destroys — which is why the hair-dryer step matters so much.

So how did they test it? With a TSI 8130 instrument, using sodium chloride aerosol — particles about 0.075 micrometres across. They tested at two different flow rates: 30 litres per minute for medical and surgical masks, and 85 litres per minute for KN95 masks, following the Chinese national standards.

The results. For bacterial filtration efficiency, or BFE, the standard requires no less than 95 percent — and the regenerated masks stayed above it.

For particle filtration efficiency, or PFE, KN95 masks must stay above 95 percent — and they did. For surgical masks, the standard only requires 30 percent; the regenerated ones averaged 92.3 percent.

And the durability question — this is the one I find most interesting. They ran five brands of masks through 10 full cycles of soaking and drying. Filtration performance barely changed. They even pushed further: KN95 masks put in an autoclave at 121 degrees for 30 minutes still held 99.2 percent and 96.6 percent.

So the answer to question two is yes. But lab tests are one thing — real wear is another. [Name] will take that.

▍S4 · 真实使用后（约 3 分钟）

Thank you. Lab tests are clean. Real life is not. So the authors ran two more checks.

First: waterproofing. The outer layer has to repel droplets. They used a Buchner funnel test: pour 100 millilitres of water onto the mask from 20 centimetres, within 20 seconds, then apply vacuum for 3 minutes, and see whether water comes through. After 10 cycles, no water seepage at all. And under the scanning electron microscope, the fibres looked the same as new — no breakage, no fracturing.

Second: actual wear. They had people wear masks for 8 hours, then treated them. For surgical masks, based on 15 samples, the filtration efficiency dropped by 0.5 to 12 percent — and the paper is honest that this varies a lot between people, and even for the same person on different days. But all 10 KN95 samples stayed above 95 percent.

They also found out why performance changes. Using a fluorescent penetrant test — basically, spraying carbon nanodots on the mask and looking at it under UV light — they showed that dirt and oil from your skin get onto the fibres and change how wettable they are. That is the real degradation mechanism — not the hot water.

Finally: did anyone actually use it? Yes. A company called Zhejiang Runtu, with over 4,000 employees, faced a mask shortage when workers returned in February 2020. Their engineers independently verified the method and made it company policy. Between the 20th of February and the 30th of March, mask use dropped from one mask per person per day to one mask every three days — saving 122,500 masks in about five weeks.

So it works in the field. [Name] will now give our verdict.

▍S5 · 验收结论 + 未证明（约 2.5 分钟）★ 最拿分的一段

Thank you. So — our verdict.

What the paper proves: a household method — hot water plus a hair dryer — can keep the filtration performance of disposable, surgical and KN95 masks essentially intact for up to 10 cycles, and it was deployed at scale, with real, measurable savings.

What it does not prove — and this is important. First, they did not measure virus killing directly. They relied on the official guidance that 56 degrees for 30 minutes inactivates the virus. Second, they tested NaCl particles, not the actual virus. Third, they only tested six products across three categories. Fourth, fit and seal — how well the mask sits on your face — was never tested; a mask that filters well but leaks at the edges protects nobody. Fifth, the sample sizes are small: 15 worn surgical masks and 10 worn KN95s.

We also found one inconsistency worth mentioning. In Section 3.5, the KN95 masks worn for 8 hours were treated with hot water. But in the Conclusions, the same result is described as treatment with pressurised steam at 121 degrees. Those are two different treatments. It does not change the main conclusion — but it is a reminder that even published papers need careful reading.

One critical point on the standard. For surgical masks, the required PFE is only 30 percent. So "we reached 92.3 percent" sounds impressive partly because the bar is low. A good reviewer always asks: impressive compared to what?

Finally, why we chose this paper. We are engineering students. This paper is not a discovery — it is a qualification campaign: define acceptance criteria, run to N cycles, inspect, then release. That is exactly what we do to turbine blades and filters. And in an emergency, the best engineering is often not the most advanced — it is the kind that works with a pot of hot water and a hair dryer.

Thank you.
关于最后一段的个人化：结尾那句 "turbine blades and filters" 是你们全场的记忆点。如果觉得太长，至少保留最后一句（"the best engineering is often not the most advanced…"），30 秒，零成本，但会让老师记住你们这组。不要删掉。

六、脱稿方法：锚点法（不是背稿）

核心原则：背稿必忘，锚点不忘。不要逐字背诵 350 词。只把每段压成 4–6 个锚点，每个锚点是一个数字、名词或画面，看到锚点就能把整句"重新长出来"。

Step 1 · 写（D4–D7）
用第五节英文稿做底子，改成你自己说得顺的话，控制在 4–6 句话。（改词不改结构：陌生的长词换成你认识的短词，出错率立刻降一半。）
Step 2 · 提锚点（D7）
给每句话挑 1 个锚点。例（S2）：
30 min / 56℃ → basin–lunchbox–thermos / 90→60→85 → UV vs pot → UV lamp spots gone → air-dry 10h = 60% vs dryer 10min = 90% → recipe
把这 6 个锚点抄到一张 A6 卡片上，字号要大。
Step 3 · 三遍制（D8–D13，每次 15 分钟）
  1. 第 1 遍（D8–D9）：照稿朗读 2 次。只求嘴熟，重点是把数字和专有名词读顺。
  2. 第 2 遍（D10–D11）：只看卡片讲 3 次。站着讲，用手机计时。忘了就看锚点，绝不允许低头念稿。
  3. 第 3 遍（D12–D13）：无卡片，站着讲 2 次。全程录音，回听一次——你会发现 80% 的问题是同一个词卡住。
Step 4 · 只背两句（关键技巧）
每段的第一句和最后一句，逐字背熟；中间随便说。
第一句背熟 → 开口不慌；最后一句背熟 → 收尾利落、交接自然。这是专业演讲者的通用做法。

十、你们独有的三个"批判性加分点"

老师判断"有没有真读懂"，就看你能不能指出论文的软肋。这三点是我在通读全文时核对出来的，务必由 S5 讲出来：

  1. 论文自身的一处不一致。§3.5 写"佩戴 8 小时的 KN95 经热水处理后仍 >95%"；但 §4 结论里描述同一批实验结果时，写的是"经 121 ℃ 压力蒸汽处理"。两种处理方法被混用了。不影响主结论，但说明原文需要细读。
  2. 标准门槛偏低。外科口罩的 PFE 国标（YY 0469–2011）只要求 ≥30%，所以"92.3%"的说服力被放大了。而一次性医用口罩标准（YY/T 0969–2013）对 PFE 根本没有要求。审稿人要问：跟什么比？
  3. 病毒灭活是"借用"的，不是测的。论文没有做任何病毒滴度实验；"56 ℃ 30 min 可灭活"直接引自国家卫健委方案。他们证明的是"过滤性能没坏"，不是"病毒被杀死了"。

把这三点讲出来，你就从"复述论文"升级成"评审论文"。这正是我们这套"鉴定评审会"框架的落脚点——前后呼应，闭环。
